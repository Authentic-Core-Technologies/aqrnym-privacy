# aQRnym privacy policy

Last updated: 14th October 2026

aQRnym is a browser extension that finds QR codes on web pages and PDFs you view and shows what each code contains. This policy explains what happens to your data when you use it.

## The short version

- QR codes are read on your own computer.
- aQRnym has no server. It does not send what it finds anywhere.
- aQRnym keeps no history and contains no analytics, tracking or advertising.

## What aQRnym reads, and where it stays

**Web pages.** To find QR codes, aQRnym looks at the pictures on the pages you visit: images, drawings, and pictures set by a page's styling. Decoding happens inside the extension on your computer. The codes it finds are kept in your browser's memory for the tab they were found in, so the extension's menu can list them. They are deleted when you close the tab or navigate away, and when you close the browser.

**Two-factor setup codes** (codes for authenticator apps) are never stored. The extension only shows that such a code exists and lets you copy it.

**PDFs.** aQRnym reads a PDF only when you click "Check this PDF". It uses the copy your browser already downloaded, reads it inside the extension, and keeps only the QR codes it finds, under the same rules as for web pages.

**Screenshots.** When you use "Scan this page" or "Select area", and when a picture on a page cannot be read any other way, aQRnym takes a screenshot of the visible part of the tab to look for QR codes in it. The screenshot is processed on your computer and discarded immediately. It is never saved or sent anywhere.

## What leaves your computer

Two things do:

1. **Requests for pictures.** When the page itself is not allowed to share a picture's contents with the extension, aQRnym asks the website that hosts that picture for it again, in the same way your browser asked for it when it showed the page. That website receives an ordinary request for the picture. It receives nothing about QR codes or anything aQRnym found.

2. **Your settings, through Chrome sync.** aQRnym's settings (whether badges are shown automatically, and the list of websites where you turned them off) are saved with Chrome's synced storage. If you are signed in to Chrome with sync turned on, Google stores these settings in your Google account so they follow you to your other computers, under Google's own privacy policy. If sync is off, they stay on your computer.

## Permissions

- **Read and change data on all websites:** needed to find QR codes on any page you visit, draw the small badges on them, request pictures as described above, and take screenshots of the visible tab when you ask.
- **Context menus:** adds the right-click entries "Read QR code", "Scan this page for QR codes" and "Select area to read QR code".
- **Storage:** saves your settings, and keeps the codes found in a tab until the tab is closed.
- **Offscreen document:** a hidden page inside the extension used to read a PDF when you ask. It is blocked from contacting any server.

## Kinds of data handled

In the terms Chrome Web Store uses, aQRnym handles the following on your computer only:

- **Website content:** pictures on pages you visit, screenshots of the visible tab, and PDFs you ask it to check.
- **Web browsing activity:** the address of the current tab, and the list of sites where you turned badges off.
- **Authentication information and personal details, when a QR code contains them:** for example a Wi-Fi password, a two-factor setup key, or a contact card. These are shown to you and can be copied; they are not stored or sent.

## Sharing and selling

aQRnym does not send any of the data above to the developer or to anyone else, so there is nothing to share or sell. The developer has no access to what you browse or what the extension finds.
